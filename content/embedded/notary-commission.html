Remote Commissioning in 5 Steps

Ontario law permits certain affidavits and statutory declarations to be commissioned by video conference. We confirm eligibility before the appointment.

STEP 01

Prepare & Email

Email the completed but unsigned document to our office for advance review, together with a copy of your government-issued photo ID. Do not sign in advance.

STEP 02

Choose How to Sign

Electronic: sign the PDF directly on an iPad or computer while using a separate phone for video.
Handwritten: print the document and sign it while the lawyer watches.
Office policy: We do not accept DocuSign for remote commissioning.

STEP 03

Attend & Verify ID

Join by WeChat video or Zoom. Show your original, valid government-issued photo ID to the lawyer on camera. Keep the camera on, confirm your location and understanding, take the oath or affirmation, and sign while the lawyer watches.

STEP 04

Send the Signed File

Immediately email the complete scanned document or electronically signed PDF to our law office. Do not make any changes after signing.

STEP 05

We Complete & Return

The lawyer completes the required remote commissioning information and normally returns the final signed copy by email shortly after the appointment.

Important: Remote commissioning and notarization are different legal processes. Some documents must be notarized in person, and we will confirm whether your document is eligible for remote commissioning before the appointment. For an eligible remote commissioning, the signer and commissioner must see, hear, and communicate with each other in real time; the signer’s identity and signature must be confirmed; and the remote jurat must record both parties’ locations and compliance with Ontario Regulation 431/20. Because receiving organizations are not required to accept remotely commissioned documents, please confirm acceptance before booking.

DOCUMENTS FOR USE OUTSIDE CANADA

Apostille & Authentication Assistance · From $500

When a Canadian public document is intended for use abroad, including in China, the receiving authority may require an apostille or another form of authentication. For countries that participate in the Hague Apostille Convention, an apostille generally replaces consular legalization. For other countries, additional legalization through an embassy or consulate may be required. Requirements vary by document, destination and recipient, and translation or other supporting steps may also be needed.

CanWise Law can review the intended use, advise on notarization where required, and assist with the Ontario apostille or authentication process for documents intended for use in China and other foreign countries. Fees start from $500; government, courier, translation and third-party charges are additional. Please send us the document, destination country and the receiving authority’s instructions for an assessment.

Please note: An apostille or certificate of authentication confirms the signature, capacity and seal on a public document. It does not certify the truth of the document’s contents or guarantee acceptance by a foreign court, government agency, school, bank or other recipient. Clients should confirm the destination country’s current legalization, translation and evidentiary requirements with the intended recipient.

Canada and China: Different Notarial Systems

A Canadian notarial act is not automatically the same as a Chinese notarial certificate.

Ontario, Canada

A notary commonly verifies a signature or certifies that a copy matches an original presented. This generally does not mean the notary has investigated every underlying fact in the document.

China

A notarial institution may examine supporting materials and issue a formal certificate concerning a legal act, fact or document. Canadian documents used in China may also need translation, an apostille or other steps.
