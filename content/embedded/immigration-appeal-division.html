Immigration Litigation & Appeals

Immigration Appeal Division

The Immigration Appeal Division (IAD) hears specific appeals involving family sponsorship refusals, removal orders and permanent resident residency obligations. Appeal rights, deadlines and available relief depend on the decision and the governing legislation.

A Merits-Based Appeal

Evidence, Testimony and Legal Argument

An IAD appeal is different from Federal Court judicial review. Depending on the appeal, the IAD may consider the correctness of the decision, procedural fairness, updated documentary evidence, witness testimony and, where the legislation permits, humanitarian and compassionate considerations.

The Minister is represented in the proceeding. A careful appeal record should address the refusal or removal grounds directly, organize corroborating evidence, prepare witnesses and explain any relevant humanitarian factors, including the best interests of a child directly affected.

Appeal Jurisdiction

Matters Commonly Heard by the IAD

Sponsorship Appeals

A Canadian citizen or permanent resident sponsor may appeal certain refused family-class applications under s. 63(1) of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act. Common issues include relationship genuineness, eligibility and admissibility. Statutory bars can remove the right of appeal.

Removal Order Appeals

Permanent residents, protected persons and certain permanent resident visa holders may have a right to appeal a removal order under s. 63(2) or (3). The IAD may allow or dismiss the appeal and, in an appropriate case, stay the removal order on conditions.

Residency Obligation Appeals

A permanent resident may appeal certain overseas determinations under s. 63(4). The case may address physical presence, the statutory exceptions in s. 28, and humanitarian and compassionate considerations.

Not every negative immigration decision can be appealed to the IAD. Temporary visa, study permit and work permit refusals do not ordinarily carry an IAD appeal. Restrictions under s. 64 and other provisions may also apply, including specified inadmissibility findings. Jurisdiction should be assessed before filing.
Deadlines and Outcomes

Act Promptly

Appeal deadlines are short and differ by category. Sponsorship and removal order appeals generally require filing within 30 days. An appeal from an overseas residency obligation determination generally requires filing within 60 days. The exact triggering date and form should be confirmed from the decision and current IAD rules.

The IAD may select a suitable case for alternative dispute resolution (ADR), a less formal process aimed at resolving the appeal without a full hearing. Selection is not automatic, and an unresolved matter proceeds through the IAD process.

An appeal may be allowed or dismissed. In certain removal order cases, the IAD may grant a stay subject to terms and later reconsideration. If a matter is returned to IRCC after a successful sponsorship appeal, further processing and admissibility review may still be required.
