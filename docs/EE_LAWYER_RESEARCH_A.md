# Express Entry lawyer-site research — set A

Reviewed November 8, 2026 (Toronto). Ten distinct Canadian law firms. Substantive Express Entry page bodies were read, not merely search snippets or homepages. Identity checks below rely on named lawyer biographies and firms' express regulatory assertions; this is not a separate law-society register audit. Firms employing consultants alongside lawyers are counted only where lawyer-led law-firm identity is established. Unified and former Matkowsky are counted once.

This is editorial research, not legal authority. Current IRCC instructions, including the November 3, 2026 changes, control over every competitor statement. Freshness is assessed per page: a current news sidebar does not update an old service page. Short quoted signals identify the problem; outdated policy is excluded from reuse.

## 1. Cohen Immigration Law — CanadaVisa

- Read: [Express Entry](https://www.canadavisa.com/express-entry.html).
- Lawyer identity: [Cohen Immigration Law](https://www.canadavisa.com/cohen-immigration-law.html) identifies Olivia Cohen as a Quebec lawyer, Ala Bujac as an Ontario lawyer, and other named counsel.
- **Current reform verified, with mixed older material.** The main page explains November 3 high-wage offers and the higher-of-two LMI calculation. Its fee table nevertheless refers to 2022, and a PNP passage uses “guarantee”; neither should be reused as current advice or assurance.
- Strength: question-led navigation, clear separation of profile preparation and post-invitation application, links to program and score explanations. Gap: length and mixed update dates make it difficult to know which detail is current.
- Reuse: a short eligibility → profile → invitation → application sequence and focused FAQ. Verify each rule directly with IRCC.

## Concrete recommendations for CanWise

1. Lead with the legal service and who it helps, then explain the three federal programs in a compact comparison. Make clear that the comparison is not an exhaustive eligibility test and FSW selection points are distinct from CRS.
2. Give the November 3 rule its own dated, official-source-backed explanation. Explain occupational wage classification, eligibility conditions and the higher-of-two calculation without importing competitor scoring tables.
3. Use four stages: assess eligibility/evidence; prepare and maintain the profile; review an invitation and score; prepare the permanent residence application. Distinguish pool waiting from application processing.
4. Group evidence by stage: language/ECA and work history before profile; full employment, civil-status, police, medical and applicable funds evidence for the application. Avoid implying every applicant has the same checklist.
5. Name concrete lawyer work: NOC duties review, work-period analysis, score/evidence reconciliation, prior refusals or discrepancies, employer evidence and response preparation. Keep representation scope subject to the agreed retainer.
6. Add short FAQs about job offers, invitations, profile updates, timelines and status. An EE profile does not itself confer or extend temporary resident status or work authorization; support this with official sources before publication.
7. Present the tracker as a historical tool after the service explanation. Label pre/post-reform data, expose source and update date, and avoid converting past cutoffs into promises.

No competitor wording should be copied. No competitor fees, success rates, predicted cutoffs or assurances should be adopted. Abrams & Krochak and Desloges were explored but not counted because accessible substantive EE detail was insufficient for this review.
