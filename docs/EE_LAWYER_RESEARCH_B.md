# Express Entry lawyer website research — group B

Reviewed November 8, 2026 (America/Toronto). These ten firms are distinct from research group A. This is a service-content benchmark, not authority for immigration rules or a professional-standing audit. Lawyer identity is supported by the firms' own profiles; no claim is made that a live law-society register was checked.

Substantive page bodies were read, rather than relying on search-result headlines. MLT Aikins blocked direct retrieval, so its primary article and profile were retrieved through Jina's text proxy. Kaminker's individual profile returned an error on direct opening; the indexed primary profile text and accessible firm team page supplied identity evidence.

The November 3, 2026 reform governs current scoring. Two entries below expressly discuss that reform; the other eight have old or undated content and are useful only for enduring presentation, service-scope and evidence themes. No conflicting third-party policy is adopted. Official IRCC instructions, current ministerial instructions and the dated official data snapshot control the implementation.

## 1. Larlee Rosenberg / Steven Meurrens

- Content: [The Return of Incomplete Applications](https://meurrensonimmigration.com/the-return-of-incomplete-applications-2/), May 6, 2026, authored on partner Steven Meurrens' professional site.
- Lawyer identity: [Larlee partner profile](https://larlee.com/lawyers/steven-meurrens/), identifying his immigration practice and British Columbia call in 2010. Counted as Larlee, not as a second firm.
- Freshness: **Old, before November 3**. It is a procedural-risk article rather than a current scoring guide.
- Strength: Concrete examples show why completeness, document validity and matching the requested evidence matter. The distinction between an incomplete return and a refusal supports more precise descriptions of legal review.
- Gap and use: Case-specific judicial outcomes do not promise a remedy for another applicant. Borrow the emphasis on evidence consistency and completeness; do not transplant legal conclusions or scoring rules.

## 2. Fasken

- Content: [Executive Transfers and Immigration, Expanding into Canada](https://www.fasken.com/-/media/6487c25d3d6a453fb31c7688752371ec.pdf), four-page chapter, particularly the permanent-residence discussion on the final page.
- Lawyer identity: [Daniel C. Lee](https://www.fasken.com/en/daniel-lee), partner with British Columbia and Alberta admissions and an economic-immigration practice.
- Freshness: **Old**. The chapter contains pandemic-era context and does not address the November 2026 reform. Its historical processing target is not a current promise.
- Strength: Separates temporary work authorization from permanent residence and presents the economic programs in an employer-oriented narrative.
- Gap and use: Too brief for personal eligibility or current CRS calculations. Adopt the clear separation of work permits and PR planning, with official sources supplying every operative rule.

## 3. Blaney McMurtry

## 4. MLT Aikins

- Content: [IRCC announces changes to Express Entry: Bonus points to reward high-wage occupations](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/ircc-announces-changes-to-express-entry-bonus-points-to-reward-high-wage-occupations/), October 20, 2026.
- Lawyer identity: [Reis Pagtakhan](https://www.mltaikins.com/people/reis-pagtakhan/), partner and corporate immigration lawyer, Manitoba call in 1995.
- Freshness: **Current-reform aware**, expressly discussing the November 3 effective date; written before commencement.
- Strength: Explains the occupation-based national wage measure, three tiers and alternative work-experience/job-offer routes. Its distinction between an individual's salary and the occupation's classification addresses a likely misunderstanding.
- Gap and use: The broad maximum-points summary does not resolve every spouse or mixed-experience calculation. Use its explanatory priorities, while official tables control the calculator and all caps. Convert future-tense implementation language to the verified current position.

## Synthesis for the CanWise service page

1. Explain three separate questions: eligibility for a managed program, CRS ranking, and receiving an invitation. A score estimate cannot determine all three.
2. Give a brief orientation to CEC, FSW and FST, and explain how an EE-linked provincial nomination relates to the system without presenting it as a fourth federal program.
3. Describe concrete legal work: assessing the appropriate route, matching actual duties to NOC, checking work evidence and language/education records, reconciling inconsistencies, and reviewing the post-invitation application.
4. Include a concise November 3, 2026 explanation with a clear checked date and official sources. National occupational wage classification is different from personal salary; current official tables determine the points.
5. Keep temporary status, work authorization and permanent-residence selection distinct. Avoid implying that entering the pool or receiving an invitation preserves status.
6. Explain completeness and updates as practical risks. Do not promise an invitation, approval or a fixed processing time.
7. Present the calculator and invitation history as supporting tools within the service journey. Mark pre-reform draws clearly so users do not treat historical cut-offs as comparable predictions.

Excluded candidates were not counted merely to reach ten: Bell Alliance's reviewed immigration service was expressly consultant-led, and McCrea did not yield sufficient substantive EE content in this review. No duplicated group-A firm is included.
