# CanWise Law Chinese Translation Rules

These rules apply to all current and future Chinese pages and translation imports.

## Case names and citations

- Preserve every case name and full case citation exactly in its original English form.
- Do not translate, transliterate, localize, or rewrite party names or `v.`.
- Do not translate parenthetical party or tribunal descriptions that form part of the reported style of cause.
- Preserve court abbreviations, years, neutral citation numbers, reporters, volume and page numbers, paragraph references, punctuation, and capitalization.
- This rule applies in headings, body text, tables, captions, links, metadata, and future Chinese-only content.
- Chinese explanatory text may appear before or after a case citation, but must not alter the citation itself.

Example to preserve exactly:

`Chen v. Canada (Citizenship and Immigration), 2023 FC 885`

## Federal Court leave terminology

- When `leave` means the Federal Court's permission for a judicial review or mandamus matter to proceed to a hearing, retain the English term and write it as `leave（开庭许可）` every time it appears.
- Apply the same form in headings, procedural descriptions, questions, and references to leave being granted, refused, or pending.
- Do not apply this rule when `leave` has a different legal or ordinary meaning.

## Judicial review terminology

- Translate the Canadian Federal Court procedure `Judicial Review` consistently as `司法复议` throughout the Chinese website.
- Do not use `司法审查` as the Chinese name of this procedure.
- Keep the English term `Judicial Review` unchanged when it is shown in parentheses or in English content.
- Keep the existing `/judicial-review/` URL unchanged.
